# Pitch evidence for CareCircle (new items only)

Researched 12 Sep 2026. This file adds to `evidence-check.md` and `nhs-plan-citations.md`; it deliberately does not repeat the HSIB 2019 radiology case, Callen 2012, NHS proxy access, NHS App results guidance, practice counts, Carers UK 2025 anxiety figures, or the 10 Year Plan quotes.

Confidence key: **verified** = figure read on the primary source (journal full text/abstract page, ONS, NHS England); **secondary** = figure seen only as cited by another source; **could not verify** = no primary source found.

Column format: theme | claim (pitch-ready) | exact figure / quote | source | confidence.

## Top 8 for slides (ranked by credibility x impact)

1. **UK results are not reaching patients.** "In a UK-wide audit of 2,572 patients, there was no record that the result had been communicated in 47% of cases, and in 31% of patients with an abnormal result." (A10, Watson/PACT, BMJ Open Quality 2024, 57 practices.) Say "no record of communication", not "never told".
2. **Access without explanation fails.** "63% of patients got their result with no explanation; 46% went and searched online." (A1, Giardina, JAMIA 2018.) Pair with A2: "only half of adults can spot which value is out of range; 38% of low-numeracy patients."
3. **Patients want immediate results, but abnormal ones cause worry when read alone.** "96% wanted immediate results, including 95% with abnormal results; 1 in 6 with an abnormal result were more worried." (A3, Steitz, JAMA Netw Open 2023, n=8,139.) This is the exact gap CareCircle's explanation layer targets.
4. **Family involvement cuts readmissions by a quarter.** "RR 0.75 at 90 days, 0.76 at 180 days, meta-analysis of RCTs in older adults." (B1, Rodakowski, JAGS 2017.) The strongest outcome evidence for the carer feature; be honest that it is discharge planning, not portal access.
5. **Shared access exists and nobody uses it.** "0.4% of portal users share access; care partners mostly log in as the patient; 57% of older patients were unaware shared access existed." (B4, Wolff 2016 + Gleason 2025.) Plus B5: older people "want to retain granular control ... simple proxy access may not adequately address the needs". Role-based consent is the differentiator, and it is evidence-backed.
6. **Trajectories beat snapshots.** "A rapid rise in frailty from a low baseline nearly triples one-year mortality odds (OR 2.84) and is visible in routine GP data months before death; a single score is 'not effective at identifying individuals'." (C6, Stow, BMC Med 2018, 26,298 people.) Plus C5: the eFI is already computed in every English GP system.
7. **Steps are a vital sign.** "Top vs bottom quartile of daily steps: HR 0.47 for death; benefit plateaus at 6,000-8,000 steps for over-60s." (C1, Paluch, Lancet Public Health 2022, 47,471 adults.) For the "decline before admission" story use C3 (34 hospitals incl. 7 UK: step count fell and heart rate rose in the days before A&E), with its caveat that only 20% had usable data.
8. **AI can explain, with a clinician in the loop.** "GPT-4 simplified 1,134 pathology reports to ~7th grade, 97.4% medically correct, 0.26% hallucinations; authors require clinician review." (A5, Steimetz, JAMA Netw Open 2024.) Balance with A6 (discharge summaries: only 54% fully accurate, 18/100 safety flags, mostly omissions). Position CareCircle as clinician-reviewed explanation, not autonomous.

Workload slide, if needed: D1 (74 min/day, 83% "wish I had seen this earlier", top wish = tool to generate result letters) and D2 (77 notifications/day, 15.5 results). UK-specific number (D3, 1.5 to 2 h/day) is secondary; the safe UK line is Elwenspoek 2020 (78% spend >30 min/day on testing) from evidence-check.md.

## Do not claim

- **Do not say "1.5 to 2 hours a day" as verified UK fact.** It appears in Watson 2022's introduction; the primary estimate was not retrieved. Label "estimated" and cite Watson 2022 if used.
- **Do not use Gresham's hospitalisation odds ratio** (OR 0.21, printed 95% CI 0.56-0.79 is internally inconsistent). Use the survival HR 0.48 per 1,000 steps and the 2-month median survival under 1,000 steps/day, and say n=37.
- **Do not cite the "38% lower hospitalisation per 1,000 steps"** as its own study; it is a secondary citation inside Gresham 2018 (Garmin, chemoradiation).
- **Do not present the UCLA heart failure Fitbit result as published** (C4): it is a medRxiv preprint.
- **Do not say "virtual wards reduce admissions" as settled.** The SE evaluation is correlational and NHS England-commissioned; the Health Foundation (Feb 2024) says it is "not yet clear", and NHS England only commissioned the national evaluation in Feb 2025.
- **Do not say "47% of UK patients are never told their result."** The PACT audit found no *record* of communication in 47%; some were told without documentation.
- **Do not say caregiver portal access cuts readmissions.** Rodakowski is discharge-planning integration; Wolff/OpenNotes shows confidence and communication gains only, uncontrolled.
- **Do not attribute Stow 2018 to Age & Ageing.** It is BMC Medicine 16:171.
- **Do not quote "9 million test result views a month"** (Global Government Forum, Feb 2026). NHS England's roadmap (Jul 2026) now says 12 million; cite that.
- **Do not claim a UK number for unpaid carers of "10.6 million" alongside Census figures without noting the source difference.** Census 2021: 5.0m E&W / 5.8m UK. Carers UK survey-based estimate: ~10.6m. Pick one and label it.
- **Carers' anxiety figures** (74% stressed/anxious) are in evidence-check.md; there is still no verified figure for carers' *information* needs about results. The nearest is B8: the 2025 BJGP review found *no research* on communicating results to carers.
