# Evidence check for pitch claims

Checked 12 Sep 2026. Verdicts: **verified** = read on the primary source; **partially** = figure confirmed but with a caveat or via a secondary source; **could not verify** = no primary source found.

Extrapolations of our own are labelled as assumptions, not evidence.

## 1. HSIB report: chest x-ray showing probable lung cancer, never communicated, patient died

| Claim | Verdict | Exact figure / quote | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| HSIB report title | verified | "Failures in communication or follow-up of unexpected significant radiological findings" | https://www.hssib.org.uk/patient-safety-investigations/failures-in-communication-or-follow-up-of-unexpected-significant-radiological-findings/ |
| Publication date | verified | Originally published **18 July 2019** (recommendations numbered 2019/039 to 2019/041; press coverage dated 18 Jul 2019). The HSSIB website PDF carries a "Date Published: 21/10/2022" stamp, which is the site migration date, not the original. Cite it as HSIB, July 2019. | landing page above; https://www.irishnews.com/magazine/technology/2019/07/18/news/patients-could-receive-texts-or-emails-following-abnormal-scan-results-1665843/ |
| Reference event | verified | 76-year-old woman, ED attendance 15 July 2017 with a heart attack. Chest x-ray reported 12 days later (27 July): "a 45mm opacity projected over the liver which may represent a right lung base mass." Letter sent to cardiology consultant, "copied to the patient's general practitioner (GP). The letters were not received and, although the email arrived, the result was not acted upon." "Neither the consultant cardiologist nor the GP received a copy of the letter." | Investigation report PDF, summary and paras 2.1.5 to 2.1.9, 4.4.9 |
| How it was discovered | verified | "Three months later, the patient went to see her GP... The GP accessed the tests taken during the patient's previous hospital admission and saw the chest X-ray report of a possible lung cancer." (20 Oct 2017) | same |
| Outcome | verified | Lung cancer confirmed on admission in Nov 2017; "The patient became progressively more unwell and died just over two months later." Died at home 31 January 2018 (about 6.5 months after the original x-ray). | same, para 2.1.17 |
| National scale statistic | verified | "Between 1 April 2017 and 14 May 2018 there were 41 serious incidents reported on the Strategic Executive Information System (StEIS)... involving a delayed lung cancer diagnosis as a result of radiological findings that were not acted upon." "In 27 incidents, informing the patient about unexpected radiological findings could have prevented serious harm." | same |
| Recommendation relevant to us | verified | Recommendation 2019/041: NHSX should develop "a method of digitally notifying patients of results" for unexpected significant radiological findings. | same |

**Pitch wording that is safe:** "In 2019 HSIB investigated the case of a 76-year-old woman whose chest x-ray showed a probable lung cancer. The report was emailed and posted to her hospital consultant and copied to her GP. The letters never arrived, the email was opened but not read, and nobody told her. She found out three months later when her GP happened to open the old result. She died in January 2018."

**Do NOT say:** "the GP ignored it" (the GP never received the letter and was the one who eventually found it), or "she died two months after the x-ray" (it was ~6.5 months after the x-ray, just over two months after diagnosis).

## 2. "20 to 62% of test results are never followed up"

| Claim | Verdict | Exact figure / quote | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Original systematic review | verified | Callen JL, Westbrook JI, Georgiou A, Li J. "Failure to follow-up test results for ambulatory patients: a systematic review." J Gen Intern Med. 2012 Oct;27(10):1334-48. PMID 22183961, PMCID PMC3445672. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22183961/ |
| The range | verified, but the pitch figure is WRONG | "Nineteen studies met the inclusion criteria and reported wide variation in the extent of tests not followed-up: **6.8% (79/1163) to 62% (125/202) for laboratory tests; 1.0% (4/395) to 35.7% (45/126) for radiology.** The impact on patient outcomes included missed cancer diagnoses." | same |
| Population | verified | Ambulatory settings: "outpatient clinics, academic medical or community health centres, or primary care practices". Literature 1995 to 2010. **"All studies were conducted in the US so care should be taken in generalising findings to other countries."** | same |

**Do NOT say "20 to 62%".** The published range is 6.8% to 62% for lab tests (1% to 35.7% for imaging), all US studies, data from 1995 to 2010. Safe wording: "A systematic review of 19 US studies found between 7% and 62% of lab results were never followed up, including missed cancer diagnoses (Callen et al., 2012)." The 2025 BJGP systematic review (Nankervis et al.) cites the same range as 6.8% to 61.9%.

## 5. Number of GP practices and appointment volumes (for the "10 to 15 results calls per practice per day" extrapolation)

| Claim | Verdict | Exact figure / quote | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Number of GP practices in England | verified (via BMA analysis of NHS England data) | "6,147 practices across England" (July 2026); "1,476 independent community GP practices have closed or merged" since 2015. | https://www.bma.org.uk/advice-and-support/nhs-delivery-and-workforce/pressures/pressures-in-general-practice |
| Registered patients | verified | "63.2 million patients registered with GP practices in England" (July 2026). | same |
| Appointments per month | verified | NHS England: "34.9 million appointments were recorded across General Practice" in July 2026 (33.9m in practice appointment books, covering 98.9% of practices). 43.8% carried out by a GP, 44.7% same day. | https://digital.nhs.uk/data-and-information/publications/statistical/appointments-in-general-practice/july-2026 (published 27 Aug 2026, official statistics in development) |
| Appointments per year / per day | verified (BMA derived from NHS England) | "approximately 386 million standard appointments were booked" in the 12 months to July 2026; "1.48 million appointments per working day" in July 2026. | BMA page above |
| Phone call volume | verified | "GP practices also received 31 million phone calls in October [2025] – more than a million each working day." "More than 8 million people submitted their GP request online during October 2025." | https://www.england.nhs.uk/2025/11/gps-managed-record-number-online-requests-october/ |

**Sanity check of "10 to 15 results-explanation calls per practice per day" (our assumption, not evidence):**
- 1.48m appointments/day ÷ 6,147 practices ≈ 240 appointments per practice per working day.
- ~31m calls/month ÷ ~22 working days ÷ 6,147 ≈ 230 inbound calls per practice per working day.
- 10 to 15 results calls per practice per day would therefore be roughly 4 to 7% of inbound calls, and ~60k to 90k calls a day nationally. That is plausible but **there is no published figure to back it**. Present it as "our estimate" and show the arithmetic, or drop it.

## Summary of what NOT to use

1. **"20 to 62% of results are never followed up"**: wrong range. Use 6.8% to 62% (lab) and say it is 19 US studies, 1995 to 2010.
2. **"Her GP ignored the x-ray"**: false. The GP never received the letter and was the one who found it three months later.
3. **"She died two months after the x-ray"**: it was 6.5 months after the x-ray, just over two months after diagnosis.
4. **A dedicated HSIB report on unfollowed blood tests in primary care**: does not exist. The nearest is the Nov 2023 HSSIB continuity-of-care report.
5. **Any percentage of GP calls being results queries**: no published figure. Label 10 to 15/practice/day as our estimate.
6. **A number for carers' information needs**: none found. Use the anxiety figures instead.
